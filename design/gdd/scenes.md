## 1. Overview

**Purpose**: Give coins a long-term use and give the game moods for different times of day.

**Scope**: The four scenes (Midday, Dawn, Sunset, Moonlit tide), buying them, previewing them, and the optional "Match my clock" mode. The sand colours are out of scope on purpose.

**Current Implementation**: A scene is a table of 16 colour tokens applied as CSS custom properties on the page, plus a `data-theme` attribute that switches decorations (stars, a low sun, the home-screen moon). `themes.ts` holds the pure rules; the picker lives in `main.ts`.

**Design Intent** (owner decisions S4-1 to S4-4):
- Scenes: Dawn, Sunset and Moonlit tide, plus the free Midday.
- Bought with coins, with a live preview before buying.
- Optional "Match my clock" (off by default); choosing a scene by hand turns it off.
- **Sand colours never change**, so puzzles and the colour-blind patterns stay valid in every scene.

## 2. Detailed Design

### 2.1 Core Mechanics

| Scene | Price | Clock hours (local, `[from, to)`) | Look |
|---|---|---|---|
| Midday | free | 9–17 | Warm cream and sea-glass (the original look) |
| Dawn | 300 | 5–9 | Soft pink sky, blue-green sea, a low pale sun |
| Sunset | 500 | 17–20 | Orange sky, purple sea, a low warm sun |
| Moonlit tide | 800 | 20–5 | Deep blue dark mode, stars, a moon on the home screen only |

**Picker**: opened from the home screen or Settings. Tapping a scene previews it live across the whole game; "Buy …" (or "Use …" if owned) confirms; closing without confirming restores the real scene. A button shows "Needs N coins (you have M)" and is disabled when unaffordable. A toggle switches "Match my clock".

**Which scene is shown** (`activeTheme`):
- Manual: the selected scene if owned, otherwise Midday.
- Match my clock on: the scene for the current hour if owned, otherwise the selected scene.
- The clock mode re-checks every 60 s while the picker is closed.
- Confirming a scene by hand sets it as selected and turns clock-matching off.

**Readability**: a unit test checks every scene's text against its background (ink on card/top/bottom ≥ 4.5:1; soft text and button text ≥ 3:1; toast ≥ 4.5:1). The browser's address-bar colour follows the scene.

### 2.1a The scene ladder (owner decisions D32, D33, D37, D39; sprint 7)

**Why:** after the three original scenes (all affordable in the first week) coins had nothing lasting to buy (GX-11). The ladder gives them a goal for months. Prices come from the 90-day coin model (`production/qa/sprint-7/economy-model.md`, re-run after D38 removed the 50-coin collection bonus).

**Price formula:** `price(n) = round50(2500 × 1.15^(n − 1))` for rungs `n` = 1…5 (2,500 → 4,350), and since D100 (sprint 13) `round50(4350 × 1.031^(n − 5))` for rungs 6…12 (4,500 → 5,400); 51,250 in total (was 72,550). The original fit is kept below for history; after D38 removed the 50-coin bonus the top rungs took 3–5 weeks each, so D100 lowered them to about 2 weeks (`production/qa/sprint-13/late-ladder.md`). Original fit (sprint 7): The three original scenes keep 300 / 500 / 800 as a first-week starter tier (D37). Fitted so that a **regular** player (8 levels and the daily a day) can afford a new rung about every 1–2 weeks (days 11, 19, 29, 41, 54, 70, 88), a **heavy** player (25 a day) still has the top 3 to chase at day 90, and a **light** player (3 a day, the daily 4 days a week) gets 3 rungs by day 82.

**Animation tiers:**
- **Tier 1, colour-only:** new colour tokens plus static CSS decoration, like the original four.
- **Tier 2, one ambient layer:** one slow background layer behind the board and home screen (falling snow, rising lanterns).
- **Tier 3, layered and reactive:** an ambient layer plus a reaction to play: a burst when a jar seals and a bigger one on a level clear.

**Rules for every animated layer:**
- It sits behind all UI, never over the board's text, and never changes contrast (the per-scene contrast test still applies).
- It stops completely under reduced motion and leaves a still frame.
- It pauses while the page is hidden.
- It stays light on battery: CSS animations or one canvas at most 30 fps, no more than about 60 particles.
- Reactions never delay input.

**Availability (D33):**
- Every scene can be bought any time and is kept forever.
- A scene whose window includes today's local date shows an **"In season"** badge in the picker.
- With **Match my clock** on, an owned *holiday* scene that is in season replaces the time-of-day scene for its whole window. If two overlap, the higher rung wins.
- Season-long scenes (High Summer, Spring Blossom, Autumn Shore, Winter Snow) only get the badge. They don't take over the clock for three months.
- Windows use northern-hemisphere seasons by default. The Settings switch **Southern hemisphere seasons** (D42, built in sprint 8) shifts the *season* windows by six months; holiday and sky windows keep their dates.

**Looks (the build brief):**
- **Sea Fog:** pale grey-green sky, misted sea, softened glass edges. A calm, low-contrast look that still passes the contrast test.
- **Winter Snow:** cool white-blue day palette, a light snow layer on the home sea curve, and slowly falling flakes.
- **Harvest Moon Lanterns:** dusk palette, a large warm moon, and paper lanterns rising slowly from the sea.
- **New Year Fireworks:** night palette with a faint ambient sparkle. A small burst on each seal, and a fuller display on the win card, in the board's sand colours.

**Win-card exception (owner decision D40, 2026-11-08):** on the win card only, New Year's display plays for about 3.5 s on its own canvas *inside* the win overlay: in front of the dim backdrop, behind the card, never over its text. Everywhere else the layer stays behind all UI.

**Names (D41):** the picker shows sentence-case names to match "Moonlit tide": Sea fog, Winter snow, Harvest moon (rung 7's full name, Harvest Moon Lanterns, is too long for the two-column picker), New Year fireworks.

**Southern hemisphere (D42, built in sprint 8 P2):** a Settings switch, "Southern hemisphere seasons" (`settings.southern`, off by default), shifts the *season* windows by six months, keeping whole months (High Summer becomes Dec 1 – Feb 28/29, Winter Snow Jun 1 – Aug 31). Holiday and special-sky windows keep their dates. The "In season" badge and Match my clock follow the switch; season scenes still never take over the clock (`themes.ts` `seasonWindow`, `inSeason(theme, dateKey, { southern })`).

**Build order:** rungs ship out of order (D39 picked one scene per tier first). The picker lists scenes by price, so the rungs that aren't built yet are simply absent until later sprints, and the prices of the built ones never change when the rest arrive.

| Scene for an hour | the scene whose `[from, to)` contains `hour`, wrapping past midnight | `hour` 0–23 | 4 → Moonlit, 5 → Dawn, 9 → Midday, 17 → Sunset, 20 → Moonlit |

## 4. Edge Cases

**Handled in Code**:
- ✅ Buying: refused, wallet unchanged, if the scene is owned, unknown or unaffordable.
- ✅ Preview without buying is undone on close.
- ✅ An unowned or unknown selected scene falls back to Midday.
- ✅ With clock matching on, a scene you don't own is skipped and the selected scene stays.
- ✅ Sand colours, patterns and unit sizes are not part of the tokens.
- ✅ The picker, home and board fit 320 px in every scene (screenshots in `production/qa/evidence/sprint-4/`).
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-08 (D17): scenes keep the shared music; no scene-specific sound.

**Not Yet Handled**:
- ⚠️ The clock uses the device's local hour (appropriate for a time-of-day look); changing the phone's clock changes the scene. Harmless, since scenes are only cosmetic. The daily puzzle follows the UTC date instead (`daily-puzzle.md`), so the two can disagree about "today" near midnight.

**Unclear**:
- None (the un-asked question is under Open Questions).
