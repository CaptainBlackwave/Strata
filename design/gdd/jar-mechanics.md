## 1. Overview

**Purpose**: Long-term variety. Every 20 levels a new kind of jar changes how the player plans.

**Scope**: The special jars (four from sprint 3, the hourglass and the sieve from sprint 13), when they appear, how they are placed, which of their states make a board unwinnable, and how the player is told about them. Basic pouring is in `pour-rules.md`.

**Current Implementation**: Optional per-jar fields on `Jar` (`cap`, `frozen`, `lock`, `funnel`) that the shared pour and seal rules respect, a schedule (`pickMechanics`), a placement step (`applyMechanics`) and UI for each.

**Design Intent** (confirmed by the owner, sprint-3 decisions, updated by D1, D8, D21, D22):
- All four mechanics ship: Narrow, Frozen, Locked lid, One-way funnel (S3-1).
- They unlock every 20 levels, easiest first: 21, 41, 61, 81 (S3-2).
- After unlock each appears in about 1 in 3 levels (S3-3); from level 45 that chance ramps to 1 in 2 by level 120 (D1, `level-generation.md` §3). Two rolled mechanics can combine from level 101.
- **Up to 3 mechanics on one level from level 101** (D22): the spare-jar swap of the D1 ramp can add a narrow or funnel to a level that rolled two. This is part of the ramp, not an accident.
- A Saturday daily features one mechanic (S3-4).
- Frozen jars thaw when any jar seals (S3-5).
- **The game says when a mechanic has made the board unwinnable**, for the cases it can be certain of (D21).

## 2. Detailed Design

### 2.1 Core Mechanics

| Mechanic | Debut | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Narrow jar** | Level 21 | An *extra* empty jar with its own capacity of 2 (`mechanics.list[narrow].cap`). It never seals (D4), so it has to end the level empty. Pours into it stop when it holds 2 units |
| **Frozen jar** | Level 41 | One filled jar (`count: 1`) starts iced: no pouring in or out. When *any* jar seals, every frozen jar thaws at once |
| **Locked lid** | Level 61 | One filled jar gets a padlock in a colour that is **not** inside that jar. It is closed until a jar of the lock's colour is sealed |
| **One-way funnel** | Level 81 | An *extra* empty jar you can pour into but never out of. It seals normally when full of one colour at normal capacity, so a funnel holding two colours is a dead end (below) |
| **Hourglass jar** | Level 101 | One filled jar counts the pours on the board; every third successful pour (`every`) its stack turns over, bottom to top. Sprint 13 M1 (D96, D97), §2.4 |
| **Sieve jar** | Level 141 | One filled jar is banded in one colour of the board: only that colour may be poured in, anything may be poured out, and it seals like any jar. Sprint 13 M2 (D96, D97), §2.5 |

**Schedule** (`pickMechanics`, the *rolled* mechanics):
- A level equal to a mechanic's `unlockAt` always gets exactly that mechanic (its debut).
- Every 10th level (tall-jar challenge) gets no mechanic before level 100. From level 100, tall-jar levels can get a mechanic with the same chance as normal levels (D8, **built in sprint 5**).
- Any other level with at least one unlocked mechanic gets one with the mechanic chance, chosen evenly from the unlocked ones. The chance is 0.34 up to level 45 and ramps to 0.5 at level 120 (D1, **built in sprint 5**; formula in `level-generation.md` §3).
- From level 101, a second, different mechanic is added with probability 0.45.
- The draw is seeded by `(mechanics.salt, level)`, so a level always has the same mechanics.

**Mechanics per level** (D22): the spare-jar swap (`level-generation.md` §2.1) runs after `pickMechanics` and, from level 60, can replace one spare with a narrow jar (or, from 81, a narrow jar or a funnel). So the most special jar types on one level are:

| Levels | Rolled | Plus spare swap | Most in total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–20 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 21–59 | 0–1 | 0 | 1 |
| 60–100 | 0–1 | 0–1 | 2 |
| 101+ | 0–2 | 0–1 | **3** (for example frozen + locked + narrow) |

**Placement** (`applyMechanics`): runs after the normal deal, with its own random stream (`mixSeed(seed, 0xa11ce)`), so a level that gets no mechanic keeps exactly the board it had before mechanics existed (fixture `tests/fixtures/plain-boards.json`). Narrow and funnel append an extra jar. Frozen and locked pick a random filled jar that is not already frozen or locked.

**Dead ends** (D21, *decided 2026-11-08, built in sprint 6*): these states can never be won, so the game reports them (`pour-rules.md` §2.1 "Dead end") even while pours remain. Only these certain cases are reported:
1. **Mixed funnel**: a funnel holds two or more colours. It can't be poured from and can't seal, so the level can't end.
2. **Lock that can't open**: a locked jar whose key colour can no longer be sealed. The certain case is when units of the key colour sit in a mixed funnel (case 1), so a full jar of that colour can never be made.
The stuck banner then says "This board can't be won now: Undo or Restart" (`feel-audio-ui.md`). Undo and Restart are free (D19).

**Player-facing**:
- First time a mechanic appears (in a level or a daily) a "New jar!" card explains it. Settings → Jar guide lists all four, showing "Unlocks at level N" for ones not met yet. If a debut level falls back to a board without its mechanic, the card waits for the next level that has it (GA-LG-6 default, `level-generation.md`).
- Tapping a jar that can't be used shakes it and shows a one-line reason.
- Thawing and unlocking play an effect and a toast ("Thawed!", "Unlocked!").
- In colour patterns mode a padlock also shows the lock colour's pattern.
- Screen readers hear each jar's state ("frozen", "locked until lagoon is sealed", "narrow", "one-way funnel"); the lock colour is spoken by its palette id.

### 2.4 Hourglass jar (sprint 13 M1, D96, D97)

*Designed for Strata; no rule, name or art is taken from another game.*

**Rules**
1. One filled jar on the board is an hourglass. It is poured into and out of like any jar and seals like any jar (full of one colour at normal capacity).
2. **The count.** Every *successful* pour anywhere on the board (into or out of the hourglass, or between two other jars) counts the hourglass down by one. A refused pour does not count, and neither do Undo, Restart, Shuffle or the extra jar.
3. **The flip.** The pour that brings the count to zero is applied first; right after it, before the next move, the hourglass's stack is reversed (its bottom unit becomes its top) and the count restarts at `every`. The flip shows the whole stack (nothing in it is buried).
4. A flip never seals or unseals anything by itself: a one-colour stack reads the same both ways, so a sealed hourglass stays sealed and an unsealed one stays unsealed. The seal rules (thaw, unlock) run after the flip as after any pour and give the same answer.
5. **Board state.** The count is stored on the jar (`Jar.hourglass = { every, left }`), so Undo restores both the count and the order, Restart returns the dealt count (`every`), and the solver, the hint plan and any saved board carry it.
6. **Shuffle.** An unsealed hourglass is a free jar: Shuffle re-deals its sand like any other free jar and keeps its count (a shuffle is not a pour). A sealed hourglass keeps its sand, like every sealed jar.
7. **Waiting.** While the hourglass holds two or more colours, *every* legal pour is useful, even one that only moves a one-colour jar into an empty jar: it brings the flip closer. So such a board is "stuck" only when no legal pour is left at all. While the hourglass is empty or one colour, a flip changes nothing, waiting can never create a useful pour, and the usual stuck rule applies unchanged.

**Placement** (`applyMechanics`): one hourglass per level that rolls it, placed after the other mechanics from the same seeded placement stream, on a random filled jar that is not sealed, frozen, locked, narrow or a funnel. Its sand is shown when the level starts (`hidden: 0`). Reason: from level 75 every dealt jar buries 3 units, so a dealt jar "with no buried units" never exists; the chosen jar is revealed instead, because a flip would otherwise bring buried sand to the top. A deal where no jar qualifies is re-dealt. Levels that do not roll the hourglass draw nothing extra from the stream and keep exactly the board they had.

**Schedule**: `{ "id": "hourglass", "unlockAt": 101, "every": 3 }` in `mechanics.list`. Level 101 is its debut and shows it alone (no spare-jar swap, D28). From 102 it is one of five unlocked mechanics in `pickMechanics`; it can combine with a second rolled mechanic and the spare swap (still at most 3 mechanics, D22). Tall-jar levels from 100 can roll it too.
- **Levels that changed.** Adding a fifth mechanic to the list changes the draw for levels 101 and up. Of levels 101–160, 16 boards changed (101, 103, 107, 110, 114, 120, 122, 125, 129, 132, 141, 145, 146, 147, 156, 158); the other 44 are byte-identical. Levels 1–100 are byte-identical (fixture `tests/fixtures/boards-1-100.json`). Of levels 101–300, 16 roll the hourglass and 15 are built with it (16 of 16 since the sieve rerolled levels 141+, sprint 13 M2); 14 of 14 since the clam rerolled levels 181+, sprint 13 M3: 101, 120, 129, 146, 172, 211, 224, 229, 231, 259, 279, 292, 296, 297).
- **Fallback.** A level that rolls the hourglass with another mechanic first falls back to the other mechanic alone (two spares), then to no mechanic, as before (`level-generation.md`). Level 170 (tall jars, frozen + hourglass) is built with frozen only: with the count in the state the solver needs more work, and long tall-jar plans go over the pour cap.

**Daily and weekly**: not yet. The Saturday rotation cycles through the four card mechanics only, so every daily is unchanged (fixture `tests/fixtures/daily-saturdays.json`, 21 Saturdays from 2026-11-07, plus a test over 520 Saturdays); no weekly theme names the hourglass, and the weekly fixture is unchanged. Task W1 adds it to both from a start date (D98).

**Player-facing (built in sprint 13 M1)**: `'hourglass'` is a `MechanicId` with its own strings, an intro card on level 101 and a Jar guide entry; the map shows its "new jar" badge at 101. `LevelSpec.mechanics` still holds only the four classic mechanics (the flag `LevelSpec.hourglass` keeps placement byte-identical) and `specMechanics(spec)` merges them for the UI. The jar wears a small pill on its base with an hourglass glyph and the pours left (3, 2, 1), in the scene's toast colours (accent at 1). A flip redraws the new order at once and turns the sand column over in 460 ms (Web Animations; none under reduced motion), plays the soft `flip` sound (haptic `[4, 24, 8]`) and announces "The hourglass flipped (jar N)". Screen readers hear "hourglass, flips in 2 pours". The "special jars" badge still counts the four classic mechanics (its text says "all four"). Evidence: `production/qa/evidence/sprint-13/hourglass/`.

**Dead ends (D21)**: none added. A flip only reorders sand inside a jar that stays open, so it never puts a unit out of reach: no hourglass state is *certainly* unwinnable on its own. The funnel and lock checks stay exact with an hourglass on the board (they count units by jar, and an hourglass is an ordinary open jar to them).

### 2.5 Sieve jar (sprint 13 M2, D96, D97)

*Designed for Strata; no rule, name or art is taken from another game.*

**Rules**
1. One filled jar on the board is a sieve, banded in one colour that is on the board (its *band*, `Jar.sieve`).
2. **In.** Only units of the band colour may be poured into a sieve. Any other colour is refused, even into an empty sieve. The usual rules still apply on top: the band colour must also match the sieve's top unit (when it has one), and the sieve must have room.
3. **Out.** Anything may be poured out of a sieve, by the usual rules (top run, room in the target). Its dealt sand may hold any colours; buried units are revealed as they are exposed, like any jar.
4. **Seal.** A sieve seals like any jar: full of one colour at normal capacity. Since only the band can enter, a sieve that is not dealt full can only ever seal as its band colour; a level ends with it sealed in its band or empty. Sealing it thaws frozen jars and opens locks of its colour, like any seal.
5. **The refusal is named.** `pourRefusal(board, from, to)` returns `'sieve'` for a wrong colour into a sieve, so the UI can explain it ("only <colour> goes in"). It is checked after the source checks and before room and top colour, because the band is the jar's own rule: a full sieve still refuses a wrong colour with `'sieve'`. The other reasons are `'same'`, `'missing'`, `'closed'` (frozen or locked), `'funnel'` (pouring out of a funnel), `'sealed'`, `'empty'`, `'full'` and `'colour'` (top mismatch); `canPour` is exactly "no reason". Every answer for a board without a sieve is unchanged.
6. **Board state.** The band is stored on the jar, so Undo, Restart, the solver, hint plans and saved boards keep it. A sieve's band is part of its solver key (`S{colour}`) and of the solver's empty-jar kind, so two empty sieves of different bands, or an empty sieve and an empty plain jar, are never pruned as duplicates.
7. **Not a pure swap.** Moving a whole one-colour jar into an empty sieve (necessarily of that colour) is a useful pour: it frees an ordinary jar that takes any colour. Moving it out of a sieve into an empty plain jar is useful for the same reason. (A narrow jar is treated the same way already.)
8. **Shuffle** (decided here): an unsealed sieve is a free jar. Shuffle re-deals its sand like any free jar's, so it may then hold any colours, as a dealt sieve can; the band is kept (a shuffle is not a pour, and the band is the jar, not its sand). A sealed sieve keeps its sand, like every sealed jar. Each re-deal is solver-proven as before.

**Placement** (`applyMechanics`): one sieve per level that rolls it, placed after every other mechanic (and after the hourglass) from the same seeded placement stream, on a random filled jar that is not sealed, frozen, locked, narrow, a funnel or the hourglass. Two draws: the jar, then a band colour uniform over the board's `colors`. The jar keeps its burial. A deal where no jar qualifies is re-dealt. Levels that do not roll the sieve draw nothing extra and keep exactly their boards.

**Band colour choice** (`proveDeal`, in the generator's solver check): the drawn band is a starting point. The generator proves up to `colorTries` bands on the same deal, the drawn one first, then the next colours in palette order (wrapping). It keeps the first band whose proof is within the level's pour cap (D36) **and ends with the sieve sealed in its band**; failing that the first band whose proof is within the cap; failing that the shortest over-cap proof (for the fallback chain); failing that the deal is rejected. All tries spend from the same solver budgets (per attempt and per step), so level starts stay bounded and identical on every device. Reason: a sieve that ends as its colour's sealed jar is the sieve doing its job (the home of that colour), and every band is solver-proven, so the board stays winnable. Measured on levels 141–300: 11 boards have a sieve and 8 of their proofs end with it sealed.

**Schedule**: `{ "id": "sieve", "unlockAt": 141, "colorTries": 3 }` in `mechanics.list`. Level 141 is its debut and shows it alone with two spares (D28). From 142 it is one of six unlocked mechanics in `pickMechanics`, can combine with a second rolled mechanic and the spare swap (still at most 3 mechanics, D22), and tall-jar levels can roll it too.
- **Levels that changed.** Adding a sixth mechanic changes the draw for levels 141 and up. Levels 1–140 are byte-identical (fixtures `boards-1-100.json` and `boards-101-140.json`, the latter hashed before the sieve at commit `1a4f9f9`). Of levels 141–300, 35 boards changed: 141, 142, 145, 158, 170, 171, 181, 182, 184, 186, 202, 216, 221, 222, 224, 225, 232, 235, 237, 240, 241, 250, 259, 262, 270, 276, 277, 278, 281, 282, 286, 291, 292, 296, 300; the other 125 are byte-identical. Of levels 141–300, 11 roll the sieve and all 11 are built with it (141, 145, 158, 170, 181, 224, 225, 232, 250, 259, 278). Since the clam rerolled levels 181+ (sprint 13 M3): 16 roll it, all 16 built (141, 145, 158, 170, 186, 201, 202, 216, 221, 222, 232, 262, 270, 276, 278, 282).
- **Fallback.** A level that rolls the sieve (or the hourglass, or both) with a classic mechanic first falls back to the classic mechanic alone (two spares), then to no mechanic. With no classic mechanic it falls back straight to no mechanic.

**Daily and weekly**: not yet. The Saturday rotation uses the four classic mechanics only (`isClassicMechanic` is false for the sieve), so every daily is unchanged (fixture `daily-saturdays.json`, plus a test over 520 Saturdays); no weekly theme names the sieve and the weekly fixture is unchanged. Task W1 adds it from a start date (D98).

**Player-facing**: not built in the core task. Until the sieve's UI task, `'sieve'` is a `BoardMechanicId` but not a `MechanicId`: `hasMechanicCard('sieve')` is false, `specMechanics` leaves it out, the Jar guide skips it (a one-line, compiler-forced filter in `src/ui/screens/sheets/guide.ts`), and the map shows no "new jar" badge at 141. The UI task adds its card, strings, band art and the `'sieve'` refusal explanation, then folds it into `MechanicId`. **The core must not ship to players without that UI**: a sieve drawn as a plain jar would refuse pours with no visible reason.

**Dead ends (D21)**: none added. A sieve never traps sand: anything can always be poured out of it, so no sieve state is *certainly* unwinnable on its own (a board can still be lost through it, for example when the only free space is a sieve of another colour, but whether space can be made elsewhere needs the solver, so the stuck rule and hints cover it, not `lostReason`). The funnel and lock checks stay exact (sound) with a sieve on the board: they count units per jar and treat a sieve as an open jar; a sieve only removes ways to win, so they never report a winnable board as lost.

### 2.6 Clam jar (sprint 13 M3, D96, D97)

*Designed for Strata; no rule, name or art is taken from another game.*

**Rules**
1. One filled jar on the board is a clam, closed under a shell. `Jar.clam` is the number of successful pours left until it opens (1 to `opensAfter`); an open clam has no `clam` field and is an ordinary jar.
2. **Closed.** Nothing may be poured into or out of a closed clam. Its sand is hidden from the player (drawn under the shell by the UI task); the core keeps the sand and its burial as dealt.
3. **The refusal is named.** `pourRefusal(board, from, to)` returns `'clam'` when either jar is a closed clam. It is checked right after `'closed'` (frozen or locked; placement never makes a clam frozen or locked, so the two never meet) and before every other reason, because a closed clam refuses everything. `canPour` is exactly "no reason". Every answer for a board without a closed clam is unchanged.
4. **Counting.** Every successful pour anywhere on the board counts each closed clam down by one, after the pour and after the hourglasses tick (the same path in `pour()`). A refused pour changes nothing and does not count. The pour that brings the count to zero opens the clam for good: the field is removed and `pour()` reports the jar in `opened` (the session's pour event carries `opened` when non-empty, for the UI to animate). Opening is not a pour: it moves no sand.
5. **Open.** From then on it is an ordinary jar: its sand is shown with its burial unchanged (buried units stay buried until exposed, as usual; the top is visible), it takes and gives sand by the usual rules and seals like any jar.
6. **Board state.** The count is stored on the jar, so Undo, Restart, the solver, hint plans and saved boards keep it. A clam's count is part of its solver key (`C{left}`) and of the solver's empty-jar kind, so a board one pour from opening is a different state from the same sand two pours away, or open.
7. **Waiting is useful.** While any clam is closed every legal pour brings it closer to opening, so `usefulMoves` keeps every legal pour, swaps included (like the hourglass "wait" rule, §2.4). The solver and the hint can therefore wait for it.
8. **Shuffle** (decided here): a closed clam keeps its sand and its count. It is shut, like a frozen or locked jar, and a shuffle is not a pour, so it does not count down. Once open it is a free jar like any other. Each re-deal is solver-proven with the count, as before.
9. **Hint**: the hint follows the solver, which never pours into or out of a closed clam and may spend pours waiting for it.

**Stuck and dead ends (decided here)**. A closed clam can block a board until it opens, but the stuck banner never fires while a pour is left that would bring it closer: with a closed clam `usefulMoves` is every legal pour, so `isStuck` is true only when **no legal pour at all** is left. That board is truly stuck, since without a pour the clam can never open. No dead-end reason is added (D21): `lostReason` treats a closed clam's sand as reachable (it opens after enough pours, and a board with no pour left is already `isStuck`), so the funnel and lock checks stay exact and never report a winnable board as lost. A closed clam never holds a sealed jar (placement picks an unsealed jar and its sand cannot change while closed), so a board is never won while a clam is closed.

**Placement** (`applyMechanics`): one clam per level that rolls it, placed after every other mechanic (after the hourglass and the sieve) from the same seeded placement stream, on a random filled jar that is not sealed, frozen, locked, narrow, a funnel, the hourglass or the sieve. One draw: the jar. Its count starts at `opensAfter`; its burial is kept. A deal where no jar qualifies is re-dealt. Levels that do not roll the clam draw nothing extra and keep exactly their boards. Every board is then solver-proven (with the count in the state) like any other.

**Schedule**: `{ "id": "clam", "unlockAt": 181, "opensAfter": 6 }` in `mechanics.list`. Level 181 is its debut and shows it alone with two spares (D28). From 182 it is one of seven unlocked mechanics in `pickMechanics`, can combine with a second rolled mechanic and the spare swap (still at most 3 mechanics, D22), and tall-jar levels can roll it too.
- **Levels that changed.** Adding a seventh mechanic changes the draw for levels 181 and up. Levels 1–180 are byte-identical (fixtures `boards-1-100.json`, `boards-101-140.json` and `boards-141-180.json`, the last hashed before the clam at commit `a30a5a0`). Of levels 181–300, 35 boards changed: 181, 186, 190, 192, 193, 194, 201, 202, 211, 214, 216, 221, 222, 224, 225, 229, 231, 242, 250, 253, 257, 259, 262, 270, 274, 275, 276, 279, 282, 287, 292, 293, 296, 297, 300; the other 85 are byte-identical. Of levels 181–300, 5 roll the clam and all 5 are built with it (181, 224, 225, 250, 259; the last four with a second mechanic).
- **Fallback.** A level that rolls the clam (with or without the hourglass or the sieve) with a classic mechanic first falls back to the classic mechanic alone (two spares), then to no mechanic. With no classic mechanic it falls back straight to no mechanic.

**Daily and weekly**: not yet. The Saturday rotation uses the four classic mechanics only (`isClassicMechanic` is false for the clam), so every daily is unchanged (fixture `daily-saturdays.json`, plus a test over 520 Saturdays); no weekly theme names the clam and the weekly fixture `weekly-boards.json` is unchanged. Task W1 adds it from a start date (D98).

**Player-facing**: not built in the core task. Until the clam's UI task, `'clam'` is a `BoardMechanicId` but not a `MechanicId`: `hasMechanicCard('clam')` is false, `specMechanics` leaves it out, the Jar guide skips it (a one-line, compiler-forced filter in `src/ui/screens/sheets/guide.ts`, as for the sieve core), and the map shows no "new jar" badge at 181. The UI task adds its card, strings, the shell art with its count, hiding the sand while closed, the opening animation (`opened`), the `'clam'` refusal explanation and making a closed clam unselectable (the board's tap check uses `isClosed`, which is frozen and locked only), then folds it into `MechanicId`. **The core must not ship to players without that UI**: a clam drawn as a plain jar would refuse pours with no visible reason and show its sand.

### 2.7 Wet sand (sprint 23 W1–W3, D189, D190)

*Designed for Strata; no rule, name or art is taken from another game.*

Wet sand is the first mechanic of the 40-level pace (D190): it debuts on level 201, the next one is chosen for 241 (D191). Unlike the jar mechanics above it belongs to **sand units**, not to a jar, so it travels with the sand.

**Rules**
1. **Wet units.** Some units are dealt wet. `Jar.wet` is a list parallel to `Jar.units` (bottom first): each entry is the successful pours left until that unit is dry (1 to `dryAfter`); 0 is dry. A jar with no wet unit has no `wet` field at all, so every board without wet sand is exactly as before.
2. **Only onto its colour.** A pour moves the source's top run (one colour, `topRun`). If **any unit of that run** is wet, the whole run follows the wet rule: it may only go onto a jar whose top is the same colour. It may never go into an empty jar, whatever its kind (ordinary, narrow, funnel, or a sieve of that band). Dry units over a wet one, or a wet one under a run of another colour, do not make the run wet: they pour as usual. (Decided here: the rule looks at the whole top run, not only at the units the pour would move. A partial pour therefore never escapes the rule because the wet unit would stay behind; one rule covers every pour, and the UI can mark the run.)
3. **The refusal is named.** `pourRefusal` returns `'wet'` when the target is empty and the moving run is wet. It is checked last but one, after `'full'` and in place of "an empty target is fine"; onto another colour the reason stays `'colour'`. Every answer for a board without wet sand is unchanged.
4. **Units carry their counts.** The units a pour moves take their own counts along; a partial pour leaves the rest of the run (with its counts) behind. An hourglass flip turns the counts over with their units.
5. **Drying.** Every successful pour anywhere on the board counts **every** wet unit down by one, the units just poured included, after the pour, the hourglasses and the clams (the same path in `pour()`). A refused pour changes nothing. At 0 a unit is dry and is ordinary sand; a jar whose last wet unit dries drops its `wet` field. `pour()` reports the jars where any unit dried in `dried`; the session's pour event carries `dried` when non-empty.
6. **Sealing waits for dry sand.** A full jar of one colour at the normal capacity (`isComplete`) is **sealed** (`isSealed`) only once none of its units is wet. Until then it seals nothing: frozen jars do not thaw and its colour's locks do not open. The pour that dries its last wet unit seals it; the session's pour event lists such jars (other than the target) in `sealedDry`, so the seal effect can play on the jar that dried. Its buried units are revealed as soon as it is complete (it is one colour either way).
7. **Winning (decided here).** A board is won when every jar is empty or complete, wet or dry (`isWon`). A complete jar holds every unit of its colour, so it can never move again; requiring it to dry first would leave the last pour able to end on a board that is fully sorted but stuck (no pour left to dry it). The winning pour then dries the board: every remaining wet unit dries, every complete jar seals and their indices are added to `dried`, so a won board is always all sealed.
8. **Board state.** The counts live on the board, so Undo, Restart, the solver, hint plans and saved boards keep them. They are part of the jar's solver key (`W{counts}`), so the same sand with other counts, or dry, is another state.
9. **Waiting is useful.** While any unit is wet every legal pour dries it, so `usefulMoves` keeps every legal pour, swaps included (as for the hourglass and the clam). The stuck banner then appears only when **no legal pour at all** is left; the board is then truly stuck (nothing can dry the sand). No dead-end reason is added (D21): wet sand never removes sand from reach.
10. **Shuffle.** Each re-dealt unit keeps its count: the shuffle pool is units with their counts, so a wet unit lands somewhere new still wet with the same number. A shuffle is not a pour and dries nothing. Sealed, frozen, locked, closed-clam and funnel jars keep their sand (and counts) as before. A complete but wet jar is not sealed, so it is a free jar. Each re-deal is solver-proven with the counts.
11. **Hint**: the hint follows the solver, which never pours wet sand into an empty jar and may spend pours waiting for sand to dry.

**Placement** (`applyMechanics`): last of all mechanics (after the clam), from the same seeded placement stream. Buried units are never wet, so a droplet is never hidden under burial. A deal with no candidate is re-dealt. Levels that do not roll wet sand draw nothing extra and keep exactly their boards. Every board is then solver-proven with the counts.

### 2.8 Tide jar (sprint 24 T1–T3, D193–D197)

*Designed for Strata; no rule, name or art is taken from another game.*

The second mechanic of the 40-level pace (D190): the tide jar debuts on level 241 (D193). Like the hourglass it is one jar with a count that every pour on the board moves, but where the hourglass turns the whole stack over, the tide **lifts it by one layer**: the sand at the bottom comes up to the top.

**Rules**
1. **One tide jar.** `Jar.tide` is `{ every, left }`, the same shape as the hourglass's count: the tide turns every `every` successful pours on the board, `left` pours from now (1 to `every`). A jar that is not a tide jar has no `tide` field, so every board without one is exactly as before.
2. **Counting.** Every successful pour anywhere on the board counts `left` down by one, a pour into or out of the tide jar included. A refused pour changes nothing.
3. **The turn (D194).** The pour that brings `left` to zero turns the tide, right after the pour: the jar's **bottom unit moves to the top** and every other unit moves **down one place** (`[a, b, c, d]` becomes `[b, c, d, a]`, bottom first). Then `left` restarts at `every`. The turn includes any sand that pour just added or took away. `pour()` reports the turned jars in `turned`, and the session's pour event carries `turned` when non-empty.
4. **No buried sand.** A tide jar holds no buried units: placement shows its whole stack (`hidden: 0`), and a turn always shows it (this only matters for hand-built boards).
5. **Turns that change nothing visible.** An empty tide jar, or one holding a single colour, looks the same after a turn. It still turns: the count restarts and the jar is listed in `turned` (as the hourglass lists every flip), so the count the player sees always runs `every … 1` and back.
6. **Sealing stops the tide (D195).** A tide jar that is sealed (full of one colour at the normal capacity and dry, §2.7) **never turns again**: its count no longer moves and is left where it was. Its field is kept, so the UI can still draw it as a tide jar, a still one. A sealed jar can never be poured into or out of, so it is sealed for good. A pour that seals the tide jar on what would have been its turning pour does not turn it. A turn keeps the jar's colours, so it never seals or unseals a jar by itself.
7. **With wet sand (§2.7).** A turn moves each unit's wet count with its unit (the same rotation of `Jar.wet`).
8. **Order (decided here).** After a pour, the rules run as hourglasses, clams, drying and then the **tide, last**. Placement keeps the tide jar off the hourglass and the clam, so only the order with drying can matter: the tide sees the board as the pour leaves it, after drying. A complete tide jar whose last wet unit dries on this pour is sealed when the tide is checked, so it does not turn (rule 6 holds exactly). A tide jar that is complete but still wet is not sealed, so it turns, and its wet counts move. A rotation and "every count down by one" commute, so the counts are the same either way.
9. **Board state.** The count lives on the board, so Undo, Restart, the solver, hint plans and saved boards keep it. It is part of the jar's solver key (`T{left}/{every}`) and of the solver's empty-jar kind, so the same sand with another count is another state.
10. **Waiting is useful.** While a tide jar is unsealed and holds two or more colours (`hasLiveTide`), every legal pour moves the tide closer, so `usefulMoves` keeps every legal pour, swaps included (as for the hourglass). An empty, one-colour or sealed tide jar adds no waits: its turn changes nothing. A whole one-colour jar poured between the tide jar and an ordinary empty jar is not a pure swap (the tide jar is its own kind, like a sieve), so it is kept. No dead-end reason is added (D21): a turn never removes sand from reach.
11. **Shuffle.** An unsealed tide jar is a free jar, like the hourglass: its sand is re-dealt and its count is kept (a shuffle is not a pour). A sealed tide jar keeps its sand. Each re-deal is solver-proven with the count.
12. **Hint**: the hint follows the solver, which plays every turn through `pour()` and may spend pours waiting for the tide.

**Placement** (`applyMechanics`): after the clam and before wet sand, from the same seeded placement stream. One filled jar that is not sealed, frozen, locked, narrow, a funnel, the hourglass, the sieve or the clam, picked uniformly; its sand is shown (`hidden: 0`) and its count starts at `every`. Frozen and locked jars are left out because their sand would move while the player can do nothing with it; narrow and funnel jars because a narrow jar never seals and a funnel never pours out, so the tide would be the only thing moving their sand; the other mechanics because each of them is its own jar. Wet sand is placed after it and may land on it. A deal with no candidate is re-dealt. Levels that do not roll the tide jar draw nothing extra and keep exactly their boards. Every board is then solver-proven with the turn.

### 2.2 State and Data

- `Jar.cap?: number`, `Jar.frozen?: boolean`, `Jar.lock?: Color`, `Jar.funnel?: boolean`. A thawed or unlocked jar has the field removed, not set to false.
- `Jar.hourglass?: { every: number; left: number }` (§2.4): flips every `every` pours, `left` pours from now (1 to `every`). Part of the jar's solver key (`C{left}`) and of the solver's empty-jar kind. `pour()` returns `opened` (indices of clams that opened); the session's pour event carries it when non-empty.
- `LevelSpec.clam?: boolean`: the level rolled the clam (kept apart from `mechanics`, like the hourglass and the sieve, so placement stays byte-identical).

Part of the jar's solver key (`W{counts}`). `pour()` returns `dried` (jars where a unit dried, including every jar the winning pour dried); the session's pour event carries `dried` and `sealedDry` when non-empty. `isComplete(jar, capacity)` is full of one colour; `isSealed` is complete and dry; `isRunWet(jar)`, `isJarWet(jar)` and `hasWet(board)` answer the wet questions.

Part of the jar's solver key (`T{left}/{every}`) and of the solver's empty-jar kind. `pour()` returns `turned` (indices of tide jars that turned, empty and one-colour turns included); the session's pour event carries it when non-empty. `riseByOne(list)` is one turn; `hasLiveTide(board)` is true while a turn would change some tide jar's sand.
- `LevelSpec.tide?: boolean`: the level rolled the tide jar (kept apart from `mechanics`, like the others since the hourglass).

- `settle(board)`: collects the colours of all sealed jars; if any jar is sealed, drops `frozen` from all jars; drops `lock` from any jar whose lock colour is in that set. Returns the same object when nothing changes.

## 4. Edge Cases

**Handled in Code**:
- ✅ Pours into or out of a frozen or locked jar, or out of a funnel, are refused like any illegal pour.
- ✅ A narrow jar can't seal, so a level can't be won with a full narrow jar.
- ✅ A whole one-colour jar moved to an empty jar is only treated as a pure swap when both jars are the same kind (a swap into a narrow or funnel jar is real progress).
- ✅ A lock never uses a colour already inside its own jar.
- ✅ Every generated board, with its mechanics, is proven winnable by the solver before the player sees it (generation timing is owned by `level-generation.md` §3).
- ✅ Shuffle never moves the sand of sealed, frozen, locked or funnel jars; the solver prunes duplicate empty jars per kind.
- ✅ Up to 3 mechanics on one level from 101 (D22): allowed by design and covered by the solver proof.

**Decided 2026-11-08, built in sprint 6**:
- **A funnel filled with mixed colours** makes the level unwinnable for good. Before D21 the stuck message appeared only once no useful move was left, so a player could keep pouring on a lost board with no signal. With D21 the stuck banner shows "This board can't be won now: Undo or Restart" as soon as the funnel holds two colours. The same applies to a lock whose key colour is trapped in a mixed funnel.

**Hourglass (sprint 13 M1)**:
- ✅ A refused pour does not count; Undo restores the count and the order across a flip; Restart restores the dealt count.
- ✅ A one-colour hourglass stays sealed (or unsealed) through a flip; the pour that seals the hourglass can also be the flipping pour, and the level is still won.
- ✅ The winning pour can be a flipping pour: a flip never changes which jars are sealed or empty, so the win stands.
- ✅ A buried stack that flips is shown whole (placement never buries the hourglass; this guards hand-built boards).
- ✅ Waiting is legal: with a mixed hourglass any legal pour is useful, so the stuck banner appears only when no pour is left.
- ✅ Shuffle keeps the count and re-deals an unsealed hourglass's sand; the solver proves the re-deal with the count.
- ✅ The solver, the hint and every generated board use the same flip rule (`pour()`); hint plans key on the count.
- ✅ No jar qualifies for placement: the deal is re-dealt; a level that never gets one falls back (§2.4).

**Sieve (sprint 13 M2)**:
- ✅ A wrong colour into a sieve is refused with the reason `'sieve'`, also into an empty or a full sieve; a refused pour changes nothing and is not counted.
- ✅ The band colour onto a different top colour in the sieve is refused as `'colour'` (the usual rule).
- ✅ Anything can be poured out, including the band colour and buried sand once exposed.
- ✅ A sieve seals only in its band (unless dealt full, which placement never picks); sealing it thaws and unlocks as usual and keeps the band.
